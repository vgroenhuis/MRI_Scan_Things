MRI geometric distortion: calibration cube

Esaote G-scan Brio (0.25 T), 3D HYCE sequence, 256 × 256 × 164 voxels of 1.055 mm. Scanned at the University of Twente, May 2022. The phantom contains small markers on a regular 3D grid with 20 mm spacing. Comparing where the scanner shows them with where they really are gives the geometric distortion, which is modelled by a 5th order polynomial.

Why a custom method? The scanner's own software can correct geometric distortion in 2D (within each slice), but not in 3D. For 3D acquisitions the through-plane component and its variation across slices stay uncorrected. A custom measurement and correction method was therefore needed to compensate for the distortion in all three dimensions.

Method

Segmentation. The volume is smoothed slightly, a slowly varying background is subtracted, and the result is thresholded. Connected components of 4–200 voxels are dot candidates; the larger bars and cubes (placed in the phantom so the scanner can calibrate itself) are rejected. Each dot's centre is its intensity-weighted centroid.

Grid identification. The dot closest to the scanner origin gets grid index (0, 0, 0). From every identified dot, the six neighbours are predicted 20 mm away, extrapolating the local step from the opposite neighbour so the search follows the distortion. The nearest candidate within 6 mm is accepted. Dots are processed in order of increasing distance to the origin, so the grid grows outwards from the well-behaved centre.

True positions. The ideal 20 mm lattice is rigidly registered (rotation and translation) to the dots within 60 mm of the isocentre, where gradient non-linearity is negligible.

Polynomial fit. Two 5th order 3D polynomials (56 terms per coordinate) are fitted by least squares: a correction that maps distorted image coordinates to true coordinates, and a forward model that maps true coordinates to where they appear in the image. Coordinates are scaled by 100 mm for numerical conditioning.

Correction polynomial coefficients

xtrue = Σ ck · (x/s)a(y/s)b(z/s)c, with s = 100 mm and (x, y, z) the distorted position in mm. Full data: data.json.
